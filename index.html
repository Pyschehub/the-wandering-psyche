Book Review — Behavioral Psychology

Thinking, Fast and Slow

Daniel Kahneman — Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2011

Kahneman's landmark work introduces two systems of thinking: the fast, intuitive System 1 and the slow, deliberate System 2. For anyone curious about why humans are so predictably irrational, this book is a revelatory read. Kahneman dissects cognitive biases with the precision of a scientist and the clarity of a storyteller — making dense behavioral economics genuinely accessible. Some chapters feel repetitive for readers already familiar with the field, but the cumulative argument is undeniable. Essential reading for students of psychology, economics, or simply human nature.

4.5 / 5

Student Research — Social Psychology

Social Media Use and Self-Esteem in University Students: A Preliminary Exploration

"Does the frequency and type of social media platform used correlate with reported self-esteem levels among undergraduate students aged 18–25?"

Methodology: A mixed-methods approach combining a 22-item online survey (adapted Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale) with brief qualitative interviews (n=34). Participants were recruited through campus channels across two universities. Platform usage was tracked via self-report over a two-week period. Data analyzed using descriptive statistics and thematic analysis.

Published Response — Identity
"I feel like I'm performing a version of myself online that doesn't really match who I am in real life. Is that normal? Does it mean I'm being fake?"

Psychological Interpretation: What you're describing is a well-documented phenomenon in social psychology — what Erving Goffman called "impression management," the way we strategically present different facets of ourselves depending on context and audience. Online environments amplify this tendency because they offer curation: we edit, delay, and frame ourselves in ways real-time life doesn't allow. This isn't inherently deceptive — it reflects our natural multiplicity.

The more important question is whether your online self is an aspirational extension of who you are, or a defensive shield against being known. The former can be generative; the latter may be worth gently exploring. You're not fake — you're navigating identity in a complicated landscape, which takes more self-awareness than most people realize.

Goffman, E. (1959). The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life. Doubleday. | Turkle, S. (2011). Alone Together. Basic Books.
