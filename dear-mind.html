Dear Mind

Anonymous questions about inner life — answered with psychology, empathy, and honesty.

Published — Identity
"I feel like I'm performing a version of myself online that doesn't match who I am. Is that normal?"

Interpretation: What you're describing is impression management — how we present different facets of ourselves depending on context. Online environments amplify this through curation, but this isn't inherently deceptive.

The question is whether your online self is an aspirational extension or a defensive shield. You're not fake — you're navigating identity in a complex landscape.

Goffman, E. (1959). The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life. Doubleday.

Published — Relationships
"Why do I keep ending up in the same kind of relationship dynamic even when I try to choose differently?"

Interpretation: Attachment theory suggests that our earliest bonds create internal working models that guide our expectations in relationships. These patterns operate beneath conscious awareness, pulling us toward familiar dynamics.

Recognizing the pattern is the first step. The second is understanding what need the familiar dynamic fulfills, even if it's not ultimately healthy.

Bowlby, J. (1969). Attachment and Loss. Basic Books.
